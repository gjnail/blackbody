Free, open-source VFX simulation

Real fire. Real water. Your shot.

Blackbody simulates fire, smoke, liquids, the sea, lava, ice and weather on your GPU, and renders them physically into live-action footage. You can place the effect in your plate, match it to the camera and render a finished composite or the passes your compositor wants.

Stock elements never match your camera, your light or your lens. Blackbody simulates the effect inside your shot, so it does.

It sits in the shot, not on top of it

  • Matches your camera. Track a handheld move with one click, or import a camera solve (.chan) or a whole USD scene.
  • Lights your scene. The fire lights the ground, the walls and the objects in the footage, shadowed by the smoke.
  • Hides behind real objects. It goes behind stand-in colliders, roto mattes or the footage's own depth pass.
  • Looks like your camera shot it. Lens blur, distortion, heat haze, highlight roll-off, and noise matched to the footage's own.

Fire and smoke

Fire that burns like fire

Fuel burns into heat, soot and flame. Hot gas rises, swirls and expands, and smoke drifts and is lit by the fire, the sky and your lights. Flame colour and brightness come from blackbody radiation, so thick flame saturates the way real flame does.

  • Measured against real fires: flame height, gas speed, and the puffing rate of a fire its size
  • Spreading fire, spot fires, flash fires, backdrafts and closed rooms that run out of air
  • Sparks, coloured flames, steam, and hoses that put fire out

Fabric

Cloth that hangs, blows, soaks and burns

Curtains, flags, towels and any mesh, made from ten real fabrics with their measured weight, stretch and stiffness. Cloth blows in the fire's own air. It catches at its ignition temperature and burns the way real cloth does: a halo of toasting, a glowing line, then char and holes that spread until it falls apart.

  • Soaks and drips in water. Wet cloth holds at 100 °C and steams in the heat
  • Casts shadows on the smoke, the ground and your footage
  • Exports as meshes (OBJ or USD) with the burnt holes cut out

Liquids

Water that refracts your footage

FLIP particles on the GPU pour, splash, sheet and break into drops, and throw off spray, foam and bubbles. The surface is ray traced as a real dielectric, so your plate bends through it, reflects in it and goes murky with depth.

  • Floating objects that bob, tip and drift as real rigid bodies
  • Viscosity from olive oil to honey, dyes, two liquids that do not mix, rain and surface tension
  • Caustics, wet ground, an underwater camera, drops on the lens and rainbows

The sea

An ocean to the horizon

A real ocean spectrum, millions of waves summed by FFT, runs on seamlessly to the horizon. The box in the middle simulates its part of the sea, so things float in it and it breaks against rocks and piers.

  • Whitecaps and foam streaks that match the wind speed
  • Beaches, reefs and surf that spills, plunges and throws a barrel
  • Tsunamis, tidal bores, tides and rivers

Lava, fire and water together

When the elements meet

Molten lava glows by its own heat as a blackbody, crusts over as it cools and lights everything around it. Fire, water and lava can share one box. Water soaks fuel and boils off in a plume of steam, and lava boils the sea while its skin chills black.

Ice, boiling and steam

Liquids with a temperature

Every particle carries its heat, with water's real heat capacities and latent heats. So water freezes into ice that floats a tenth out of the water. It boils in nucleate streams, skates on its own vapour past the Leidenfrost point, and evaporates into steam that clouds in cold air.

Weather and clouds

From a snowflake to a storm

Snow, sleet, freezing rain, graupel and hail fall as the column of air above decides. Each piece melts, refreezes and settles where it lands. The sky can be simulated too, with clouds building from the sun-warmed ground into storms that rain and hail.

The app

Built for working on a shot

The simulation runs live while you change it, and finished frames are cached as you go. Every setting has a tooltip and can be scrubbed by dragging its name.

Or build anything

Blackbody opens on an empty stage with a camera of your own. You can put in fire, water, cloth, smoke, weather, forces and objects, and move them with a 3D gizmo. Then right-click to set things on fire, make them float, soak them, attach them to each other or send them along a path. When it works, the Shot workspace puts it into your footage.

Layers

Several effects in one shot

A campfire in front and a waterfall behind, each its own simulation with its own box and scale, composited back to front.
Roto

Shapes in front of the effect

Draw shapes around what is in front of the effect and key them over the shot. The effect goes behind them, in the viewer and in renders.
Build and Shot

Two workspaces

Build is your own stage and camera for making the effect. Shot is the shot's camera, with your footage, roto and layers. Tab switches between them.

Outputs

Whatever your pipeline needs

Multi-layer OpenEXR

Nuke · After Effects · Fusion · Resolve · Flame

Scene-linear, premultiplied RGBA with emission, glow, heat, depth, fire light, holdout, scorch, soot and wet layers, plus the liquid's mattes.

Deep OpenEXR

Nuke · Houdini · Katana

Up to 8 samples per pixel with front and back depth, so fire and smoke merge correctly with other deep renders.

ProRes 4444 and PNG elements

Premiere · Final Cut · Resolve · any editor

An element with alpha that drops onto your footage, premultiplied or straight.

Finished composite

Delivery and review

ProRes 422 HQ, H.264, H.265 or DNxHR over the footage, with the footage's audio carried along.

OpenVDB volumes

Blender · Houdini · Maya · Cinema 4D · Unreal

Density, temperature, flame, fuel and velocity grids, and the liquid as a level set.

Liquid and fabric meshes

USD · OBJ

The liquid surface with per-point velocities for motion blur, spray, foam and bubbles as points, and cloth with its burnt holes.

Under the hood

Physics, not tricks

Everything runs in GPU compute shaders through wgpu, so it runs on Vulkan, Direct3D 12 and Metal. Nearly 300 tests check the physics against textbook values and measurements of real fires, waves and weather.

Get Blackbody

Up and running in a few minutes

You need Python 3.10 or newer and a GPU from the last eight years. The first run sets up a private environment, which is about a 400 MB download.

Windows

Clone or download the repository, then double-click Blackbody.bat.

git clone https://github.com/gjnail/blackbody.git
cd blackbody
Blackbody.bat

macOS and Linux

The same code runs from source. It has not been tested on these systems yet, so reports are welcome.

git clone https://github.com/gjnail/blackbody.git
cd blackbody
./blackbody.sh
