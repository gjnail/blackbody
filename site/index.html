Free, open-source VFX simulation

Real fire. Real water. Your shot.

Blackbody simulates fire, smoke, liquids, the sea, lava, cloth, things that fall and break, explosions, lightning, sand, snow, grass and weather on your GPU. Build an effect from nothing or start from {{preset_count}} presets, line it up with your footage, track the camera, and render a finished composite or the passes your compositor wants.

Stock elements never match your camera, your light or your lens. Blackbody simulates the effect inside your shot, so it does.

{{video|ui-lineup||Lining up the ground}}

Line up the ground in four drags

Drag a grid's corners onto anything rectangular lying on the ground in the footage: a paving slab, a rug, a parking bay. Blackbody works out the lens from the vanishing points and the camera's tilt, roll and height from the rectangle, and draws a perspective grid, the horizon and a 1.75 m figure over the footage to check. From then on the effect stands on the real ground at its real size.

  • No rectangle in view? Line up from the horizon.
  • Sloping ground? Two upright lines keep the world level, and the slope becomes solid ground.
  • The lens recorded in the file (photos, phone video) is used by itself.
Lining up the ground →
{{video|tracked_shot||A tracked dolly shot}}

Track any camera move, in metres

Tracking › Track (Ctrl+T) follows about 60 spots through the footage to a fraction of a pixel, drops the ones that move by themselves, and works out where the camera is and where it looks on every frame: a pan, a dolly, a walk, a car or a drone. The ground gives the move in real metres, and a bundle adjustment keeps errors from piling up along it. The effect stays put on the ground.

  • Reports how closely it fits, in pixels
  • Or import a solve from your matchmover (.chan), or a whole USD scene with its camera, objects and lights

It sits in the shot, not on top of it

  • Meets real surfaces. Line up a wall, a table, a ramp or stairs in the footage: water runs down it, smoke flows round it, fire spreads over it, and it hides what is behind it.
  • Lights your scene. The fire lights the ground, the walls and the objects in the footage, shadowed by the smoke.
  • Hides behind real objects. It goes behind stand-in colliders, roto mattes or the footage's own depth pass.
  • Looks like your camera shot it. Lens blur, distortion, heat haze, highlight roll-off, and noise matched to the footage's own.

Put it in, move it, make it happen

Start from the card on the empty stage or from Create's {{block_count}} building blocks. A 3D gizmo moves, stretches and turns what you select, with snapping. Then right-click it for what it can do:

  • Set it on fire, make it fall, make it breakable, hang it on a rope or a spring, hinge it
  • Make it float, sink, hot or freezing, hollow it out, soak a cloth or let it go
  • Pour sand from it or change what it is made of, turn a source into fire, water, lava or a force
  • Attach it to something, or send it along a path you click out on the ground

Fire and smoke

Fire that burns like fire

Fuel burns into heat, soot and flame. Hot gas rises, swirls and expands, and smoke drifts and is lit by the fire, the sky and your lights. Flame colour and brightness come from blackbody radiation, so thick flame saturates the way real flame does.

  • Measured against real fires: flame height, gas speed, and the puffing rate of a fire its size
  • Spreading fire, spot fires, flash fires, backdrafts and closed rooms that run out of air
  • Sparks, coloured flames, steam, and hoses that put fire out

Fabric

Cloth that hangs, blows, holds, tears and burns

Curtains, flags, towels and any mesh, made from ten real fabrics with their measured weight, stretch and stiffness. Cloth blows in the fire's own air. It catches at its ignition temperature and burns the way real cloth does: a halo of toasting, a glowing line, then char and holes that spread until it falls apart. It holds what lands on it, and tears when it cannot: a crate dropped onto a sheet laced into a frame rests in it, and a steel ball dropped beside it rips a ragged hole that the crate falls through after it.

  • Soaks and drips in water. Wet cloth holds at 100 °C and steams in the heat
  • Tears where its threads are pulled past their breaking stretch, as each fabric does: the rip runs on as a ragged slit
  • Holds what falls on it: crates rest in a sheet, and sand poured onto one heaps in the dip it makes
  • Hangs from its top edge, a side or its corners, or is held all round its edges like a trampoline
  • Casts shadows on the smoke, the ground and your footage
  • Exports as meshes (OBJ or USD) with the burnt holes cut out

Things that fall

Objects that fall, tumble, burn and break

Any object can be a rigid body: it drops, slides, bounces, stacks and knocks things over, in real materials with their handbook weight, friction and bounce. The smoke, the water and the wind push it about. A burning one keeps burning as it tumbles, and anything attached to it goes along. Things break: walls at the mortar, glass into shards, pots into pieces, in a puff of dust. And they hang on ropes, springs and hinges.

  • Wood, stone, brick, concrete, steel, glass, ice, rubber and more, drawn in CG
  • Hundreds of pieces when something breaks, pushing the smoke and the water
  • Things that break and burn piece by piece, charring until they fall in
  • Ropes, steel cables, springs, hinges and ball joints that snap or tear out
  • A stage without footage: a floor to the horizon, lit by the fire, or path traced with Lume
  • Into your footage with their shadows on the real ground

Explosions

A blast wave that throws and breaks

A source's Blast is an explosive charge in kilograms of TNT. When it goes off, its blast wave throws the things that fall, blows breakable things apart piece by piece and scatters sand and snow, by the impulse a blast that size gives at that distance, while the fireball and the smoke column rise. The viewer marks each charge, its fireball's size and when it goes off.

Lightning

A bolt that flashes, lights the set and sets fire

Lightning is a light: a bolt from where it starts to where it strikes, jagged at every scale, with branches that light only in its first flash. It flashes a few times down the same channel, each stroke fading in tens of milliseconds, lights the set and the smoke like a row of lamps, and starts a fire where it strikes.

Ramps, wheels and motors

Things that tilt, roll and drive

Any object can be tilted and rolled as well as turned: a plank becomes a ramp, a wall leans, a wheel stands on its rim. Things slide down a slope steeper than their friction angle and stay put on a gentler one. A hinge can have a motor, held at a speed with the strength you give it: wheels drive a cart along, a turntable spins up until what is on it flies off, a windmill's sails stir the smoke.

ClassicLume

Lume lighting

A path-traced stage

Turn on Lume and the set drawn in CG is path traced: light bounces from surface to surface, so a fire inside a shed lights its walls and the light they throw back fills it. Every fire light, glowing patch of hot metal and lamp casts its own shadow, as soft as the light is big, through the objects and the smoke. An HDRI's sun casts sharp shadows, and glass, ice and jelly bend and tint what is seen through them. Measured against a research path tracer on test scenes, its mean brightness is within about 1%, and the viewer sharpens over a few seconds once you stop.

Sand, snow and mud

Stuff that piles, packs, slumps and wobbles

Sand, snow, mud, jelly and clay are hundreds of thousands of particles that each remember how they have been squeezed and stretched. Sand pours and heaps at its angle of repose, snow packs into snowballs that stick where they are thrown, mud flows until it is thin enough to stop, jelly springs back and clay stays squashed. Things that fall land on it or sink into it, objects plough through it, and cloth catches it or drapes over it. Water gets into sand and carries it off: a pour digs a gully down a heap, and a sand castle the water reaches soaks through and slumps. Snow melts where flames touch it; wax, chocolate and metal melt in the fire and set again, and molten iron glows and lights the floor round it.

  • Seven materials, from dry sand to jelly, with their real behaviour
  • Bodies of it, or streams poured from a nozzle
  • Two-way with falling objects: a crate rests on sand where a steel ball sinks in
  • Two-way with water: sand gets damp, soaks through and is carried off
  • Heat by touch: chocolate melts in a hot steel pan in moments and on a wooden board more slowly; snow melts on anything warmer than freezing
  • Heat from the fire: wax, chocolate and metal melt and set again as they cool; hot metal glows and lights the floor
  • Sticky: packing snow thrown at a wall stays on it, mud clings to a crate, dry sand runs off
  • Fire: dry leaves, sawdust and coal catch, feed the flames and burn down to ash
  • Cloth: a sheet catches sand poured onto it and sags, or drapes over a heap
  • Drawn in CG: glinting grains, snow that lets the light in, clear jelly

Grass and plants

Every blade bends, and burns

Lawns, long grass, wheat and reeds are patches of blades, each one simulated. They bend in the wind in rolling waves, lean into a fire's draught, part round what moves through them and spring back. In a dry field the fire runs on by itself, faster downwind, each blade catching, burning down to stubble and feeding the flames as it goes.

  • Four kinds, from a thick lawn to reeds taller than a person
  • On the ground or up a hillside, but never through a crate
  • Fire spreads through it on its own fuel; fresh grass is hard to light
  • Lit like the smoke, glowing where the sun is behind it

Text and logos

Your words, on fire

Type words in any font on your computer and they become solid 3D letters that work like any other object. Burning text has fire all over the letters. Text is solid: set it on fire, drop it, smash it, float it. Water letters hang in the air, then fall and splash. Logos and pictures work the same way, traced from an SVG, PNG or JPG.

  • Change the words, the font or the size later, and the fire changes with them
  • Invert and threshold for pictures with no transparency

Liquids

Water that refracts your footage

FLIP particles on the GPU pour, splash, sheet and break into drops, and throw off spray, foam and bubbles. The surface is ray traced as a real dielectric, so your plate bends through it, reflects in it and goes murky with depth.

  • Floating objects that bob, tip and drift as real rigid bodies
  • Viscosity from olive oil to honey, dyes, two liquids that do not mix, rain and surface tension
  • Caustics, wet ground, an underwater camera, drops on the lens and rainbows

The sea

An ocean to the horizon

A real ocean spectrum, millions of waves summed by FFT, runs on seamlessly to the horizon. The box in the middle simulates its part of the sea, so things float in it and it breaks against rocks and piers.

  • Whitecaps and foam streaks that match the wind speed
  • Beaches, reefs and surf that spills, plunges and throws a barrel
  • Tsunamis, tidal bores, tides and rivers

Lava, fire and water together

When the elements meet

Molten lava glows by its own heat as a blackbody, crusts over as it cools and lights everything around it. Fire, water and lava can share one box. Water soaks fuel and boils off in a plume of steam, and lava boils the sea while its skin chills black.

Ice, boiling and steam

Liquids with a temperature

Every particle carries its heat, with water's real heat capacities and latent heats. So water freezes into ice that floats a tenth out of the water. It boils in nucleate streams, skates on its own vapour past the Leidenfrost point, and evaporates into steam that clouds in cold air.

Build and Shot

Two workspaces

Build is your own stage and camera for making the effect. Shot is the shot's camera, with your footage, roto and layers. Tab switches between them.
Selection

Several things at once

Box-select, group, duplicate, and repeat in a row, a ring or scattered, with what is attached coming along.
Blocks

Your own blocks

Save what you built under Yours in Create, as one file with its meshes, to add to any scene or share.
Layers

Several effects in one shot

A campfire in front and a waterfall behind, each its own simulation with its own box and scale, composited back to front.
Roto

Shapes in front of the effect

Draw shapes around what is in front of the effect and key them over the shot. The effect goes behind them, in the viewer and in renders.
Timing

When things happen

Each source, pour and let-go as a bar on the timeline: drag it in time, or drag its ends.

Outputs

Whatever your pipeline needs

Multi-layer OpenEXR

Nuke · After Effects · Fusion · Resolve · Flame

Scene-linear, premultiplied RGBA with emission, glow, heat, depth, fire light, holdout, scorch, soot and wet layers, plus the liquid's mattes.

Deep OpenEXR

Nuke · Houdini · Katana

Up to 8 samples per pixel with front and back depth, so fire and smoke merge correctly with other deep renders.

ProRes 4444 and PNG elements

Premiere · Final Cut · Resolve · any editor

An element with alpha that drops onto your footage, premultiplied or straight.

Finished composite

Delivery and review

ProRes 422 HQ, H.264, H.265 or DNxHR over the footage, with the footage's audio carried along.

OpenVDB volumes

Blender · Houdini · Maya · Cinema 4D · Unreal

Density, temperature, flame, fuel and velocity grids, and the liquid as a level set.

Liquid and fabric meshes

USD · OBJ

The liquid surface with per-point velocities for motion blur, spray, foam and bubbles as points, and cloth with its burnt holes.

Under the hood

Physics, not tricks

Everything runs in GPU compute shaders through wgpu, so it runs on Vulkan, Direct3D 12 and Metal. More than 300 tests check the physics against textbook values and measurements of real fires, waves, falls, sand and weather.

Get Blackbody

Up and running in a few minutes

You need Python 3.10 or newer and a GPU from the last eight years. The first run sets up a private environment, which is about a 400 MB download.

Windows

Clone or download the repository, then double-click Blackbody.bat.

git clone https://github.com/gjnail/blackbody.git
cd blackbody
Blackbody.bat

macOS and Linux

The same code runs from source. It has not been tested on these systems yet, so reports are welcome.

git clone https://github.com/gjnail/blackbody.git
cd blackbody
./blackbody.sh
